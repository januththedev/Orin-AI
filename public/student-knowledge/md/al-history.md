## AL History: Sri Lanka, India, Europe and the modern world: What is corroboration in historical research?

It means comparing evidence from different sources to assess agreement, difference and reliability. Sources may depend on each other, so several retellings of the same original account do not necessarily provide independent corroboration.

## AL History: Sri Lanka, India, Europe and the modern world: How can I assess a historical source's purpose?

Ask who produced it, for whom, under what circumstances and with what interests. Purpose affects what is emphasised or omitted. Identifying a bias does not make a source useless; it changes how and for what question it can be used.

## AL History: Sri Lanka, India, Europe and the modern world: How do a trigger and an underlying cause differ?

A trigger is an immediate event associated with the outbreak of a process. Underlying causes are longer-term conditions that made that process possible or more likely. Explain the links and relative importance using the prescribed period's evidence.

## AL History: Sri Lanka, India, Europe and the modern world: How can a comparative history paragraph be structured?

Choose one criterion, make a claim about both cases, give evidence for each, then explain the similarity or difference and its significance. Two unrelated narratives placed side by side are not yet a comparison.

## AL History: Sri Lanka, India, Europe and the modern world: How should I evaluate a historical interpretation?

Identify its claim, evidence, assumptions and treatment of alternatives. Compare it with relevant primary material and other interpretations. Avoid inventing historians' quotations or assuming that a later publication is automatically more reliable.
