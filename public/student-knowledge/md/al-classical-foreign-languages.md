## AL Classical and foreign languages: study methods: How can I check whether a language resource matches my A/L subject?

Confirm the language, examination year, script, prescribed texts and syllabus edition. An archival subject list establishes historical offerings, not automatic current eligibility. Different language subjects require different grammar and cultural knowledge.

## AL Classical and foreign languages: study methods: How should an inflected language be studied?

Learn forms together with their functions in authentic sentences: for example, how case, number, gender or tense operates in that language. Do not transfer a rule from one language simply because another language uses a similar label.

## AL Classical and foreign languages: study methods: What is a useful translation workflow?

Establish the passage's context, identify sentence structure, resolve unfamiliar forms and produce a natural translation that preserves meaning. Recheck pronouns, tense, negation and register. Keep uncertain readings explicit rather than concealing them with fluent invented detail.

## AL Classical and foreign languages: study methods: How can I practise listening or pronunciation?

Use reliable recordings or instruction for the actual language variety being studied, repeat short segments and compare your production with feedback. A written transliteration alone cannot encode every sound contrast, rhythm or tone accurately.

## AL Classical and foreign languages: study methods: How do I analyse a classical source responsibly?

Identify the text, edition, genre and translation used. Separate the source's wording from commentary and later interpretation. Avoid fabricated quotations, invented word etymologies and claims that one brief gloss captures every historical use.

## AL Classical and foreign languages: study methods: What belongs in a language error log?

Record the original error, a checked correction, the underlying rule, an original example and a later recall date. Group recurring issues such as agreement, particles, word order or character formation according to the language, not a universal invented grammar.
