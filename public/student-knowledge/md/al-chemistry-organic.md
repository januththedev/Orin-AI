## AL Chemistry: organic structures and reactions: What is a functional group?

It is an atom or group of atoms associated with characteristic chemical behaviour, such as a hydroxyl group in an alcohol or a carboxyl group in a carboxylic acid. The rest of the structure and reaction conditions still influence reactivity.

## AL Chemistry: organic structures and reactions: How do structural isomers differ?

They have the same molecular formula but different connectivity. For example, different arrangements can produce distinct functional groups or carbon skeletons. Do not confuse structural isomerism with different spatial arrangements of the same connectivity.

## AL Chemistry: organic structures and reactions: Why can alkenes undergo addition reactions?

Their carbon-carbon double bond includes a π bond that can be broken while new bonds form to added atoms or groups. Specify reagents and conditions. A product prediction without conditions may be incomplete or ambiguous.

## AL Chemistry: organic structures and reactions: What is the difference between substitution and elimination?

Substitution replaces an atom or group with another. Elimination removes groups from adjacent atoms to form a multiple bond in a common school-level case. Substrate, reagent, solvent and temperature can affect which pathway predominates.

## AL Chemistry: organic structures and reactions: What happens in esterification of a carboxylic acid and an alcohol?

Under suitable acid-catalysed conditions, they can form an ester and water in an equilibrium reaction. Name both organic reactants and the ester carefully. This is a reaction principle, not an unsupervised laboratory procedure.

## AL Chemistry: organic structures and reactions: How should I solve an organic reaction sequence?

Track the carbon skeleton and functional groups at each step, add the required reagent and conditions, and check whether oxidation state or chain length changes. Use structures rather than relying only on names. Verify atom counts and plausible products.
