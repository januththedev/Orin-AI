## AL English: literature, argument and language: How do I build an argument about a literary text?

State an interpretation, support it with accurately chosen details and explain how those details support the claim. Consider a plausible alternative reading. A quotation is evidence only when its relevance is explained.

## AL English: literature, argument and language: What is dramatic irony?

It occurs when an audience knows something important that a character does not, shaping the significance of the character's words or actions. Explain the particular knowledge gap and its effect rather than merely labelling an example.

## AL English: literature, argument and language: How do tone and mood differ?

Tone concerns an attitude or stance conveyed by a voice or text. Mood concerns an atmosphere or feeling created for a reader or audience. They can interact, and an interpretation needs evidence from language and structure.

## AL English: literature, argument and language: How should an unseen passage be approached?

Read once for situation and overall movement, then annotate significant choices and changes. Develop a central interpretation and select evidence. Avoid forcing a memorised theme onto a passage that does not support it.

## AL English: literature, argument and language: How can I integrate a short quotation?

Make it part of a grammatical sentence and follow it with analysis. Quote only the necessary words and represent the original accurately. If you paraphrase, keep the meaning and acknowledge the source as the task requires.

## AL English: literature, argument and language: How does English as an A/L subject differ from General English?

They are distinct examination subjects with different aims and prescribed requirements. Confirm the exact course and syllabus before choosing materials. This dataset's literary-analysis methods do not establish a current prescribed-text list for either course.
