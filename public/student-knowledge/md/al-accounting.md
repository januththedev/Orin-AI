## AL Accounting: transactions and financial statements: What is the double-entry principle?

Every recorded transaction has equal total debit and credit amounts in the relevant accounts. Debit and credit do not universally mean decrease and increase. Their effects depend on the account type and the transaction.

## AL Accounting: transactions and financial statements: How is straight-line depreciation calculated in a simple example?

For cost LKR 100,000, residual value LKR 10,000 and useful life 5 years, annual depreciation is (100,000 − 10,000)/5 = LKR 18,000. The example assumes a full year and a simple constant annual charge.

## AL Accounting: transactions and financial statements: What is the accrual basis?

Income and expenses are recognised in the periods to which they relate under the applicable rules, rather than solely when cash moves. Distinguish accrued expenses, prepayments and cash transactions using a timeline.

## AL Accounting: transactions and financial statements: How do gross profit and net profit differ?

Gross profit compares revenue with cost of sales. Further operating and other relevant items affect the final profit measure. State which profit measure is being calculated and follow the presentation and standards required by the syllabus.

## AL Accounting: transactions and financial statements: Why might a bank statement and cash book show different balances?

Timing differences, unpresented payments, deposits in transit, bank charges or errors can create differences. Update the cash book for appropriate bank-originated items, then prepare the reconciliation using the stated balance convention.

## AL Accounting: transactions and financial statements: Does a balanced trial balance prove that every transaction is correct?

No. Errors such as complete omission, certain incorrect-account entries or equal compensating errors can leave total debits and credits equal. A trial balance is a useful check, not proof of complete accuracy.
