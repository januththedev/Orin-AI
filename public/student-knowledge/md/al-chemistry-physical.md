## AL Chemistry: moles, equilibrium and energetics: How many moles are in 5.85 g of NaCl using molar mass 58.5 g mol⁻¹?

n = m/M = 5.85/58.5 = 0.100 mol. Use the molar mass specified or calculated from the question's atomic-mass data. A mole counts entities; it is not itself a mass unit.

## AL Chemistry: moles, equilibrium and energetics: What is the concentration of 0.20 mol in a final solution volume of 0.50 dm³?

c = n/V = 0.20/0.50 = 0.40 mol dm⁻³. Use the final solution volume, not automatically the volume of solvent added. Convert cubic centimetres to cubic decimetres before substitution.

## AL Chemistry: moles, equilibrium and energetics: What does dynamic equilibrium mean?

In a closed system at equilibrium, forward and reverse reaction rates are equal while macroscopic properties remain constant. Reactant and product concentrations need not be equal. Both reactions continue at the microscopic level.

## AL Chemistry: moles, equilibrium and energetics: How does a catalyst affect an equilibrium system?

A catalyst provides a different pathway with lower activation barriers and helps equilibrium be reached faster. At a fixed temperature it does not change the equilibrium constant or the equilibrium composition of the same reaction.

## AL Chemistry: moles, equilibrium and energetics: How do exothermic and endothermic changes differ?

An exothermic reaction has negative reaction enthalpy under the stated convention and transfers energy to its surroundings. An endothermic reaction has positive reaction enthalpy and absorbs energy. Distinguish the system from the surroundings when discussing temperature changes.

## AL Chemistry: moles, equilibrium and energetics: Is pH = −log₁₀[H⁺] always an exact definition?

It is a useful school-level dilute-solution approximation when concentration is expressed appropriately. The more precise definition uses hydrogen-ion activity. State the assumptions in numerical examples and do not extend dilute-solution shortcuts to arbitrary concentrated mixtures.
