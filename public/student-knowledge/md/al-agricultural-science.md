## AL Agricultural Science: crops, soils and livestock: How do soil pH and nutrient availability interact?

Soil pH influences chemical forms, microbial processes and availability of several nutrients. Requirements vary by crop and soil. A deficiency symptom alone is not a reliable soil diagnosis; use suitable testing and local agronomic advice.

## AL Agricultural Science: crops, soils and livestock: What is integrated pest management?

It combines monitoring and appropriate cultural, biological, physical and, when justified, chemical methods to manage pests. Decisions should consider thresholds and environmental effects. This pack does not supply pesticide dosages or replace registered instructions and qualified guidance.

## AL Agricultural Science: crops, soils and livestock: What does irrigation efficiency depend on?

It depends on how much supplied water contributes to the intended crop-water need, considering losses and the definition used. Soil, crop, climate, scheduling and equipment matter. A technology is not automatically efficient in every setting.

## AL Agricultural Science: crops, soils and livestock: A trial plot gives 40 kg from 20 m². What is the simple area-based yield?

Yield is 40/20 = 2 kg m⁻². Scaling to a hectare gives 20,000 kg ha⁻¹ only if the plot is representative and the same area basis is appropriate. Distinguish measured results from extrapolated estimates.

## AL Agricultural Science: crops, soils and livestock: Why is livestock biosecurity important?

It aims to reduce introduction and spread of disease through suitable management of animals, people, equipment and movement. Measures depend on species and local requirements. Consult qualified veterinary guidance rather than using generic study notes to treat an animal.

## AL Agricultural Science: crops, soils and livestock: How should an agricultural experiment be evaluated?

Check the question, controls, replication, measurement quality and variability. Compare treatments using appropriate data, note confounding factors and state limits of inference. One successful plant is not enough to establish that a treatment is generally superior.
