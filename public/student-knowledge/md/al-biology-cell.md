## AL Biology: cells, biomolecules and enzymes: How do prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells differ?

Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus and the typical membrane-bound organelles of eukaryotes. Both have genetic material, ribosomes, cytoplasm and a plasma membrane. Do not describe a prokaryote as having no cellular organisation or no DNA.

## AL Biology: cells, biomolecules and enzymes: What is the fluid-mosaic model?

It describes a biological membrane as a dynamic lipid bilayer with associated proteins and other components. Different molecules move differently, and membranes are selectively permeable. A membrane is not simply an inert bag or an unrestricted sieve.

## AL Biology: cells, biomolecules and enzymes: How do diffusion, osmosis and active transport differ?

Diffusion is net movement down a concentration gradient. Osmosis concerns net water movement across a selectively permeable membrane in relation to water potential. Active transport uses energy directly or indirectly to move substances against relevant gradients.

## AL Biology: cells, biomolecules and enzymes: Why does temperature affect enzyme activity?

Temperature can alter molecular collision rates and protein structure. Activity commonly rises over a range and then falls as the enzyme loses its functional structure. The optimum depends on the enzyme and conditions; not every enzyme has the same optimum temperature.

## AL Biology: cells, biomolecules and enzymes: What is the difference between DNA and RNA?

DNA commonly stores hereditary information and has deoxyribose; RNA has ribose and diverse roles in gene expression and regulation. DNA typically uses thymine while RNA typically uses uracil. Avoid assuming that every RNA molecule is a short-lived messenger.

## AL Biology: cells, biomolecules and enzymes: Why are ATP and glucose not interchangeable terms?

Glucose is an organic molecule that can serve as a metabolic fuel or building block. ATP couples energy-releasing and energy-requiring cellular processes. Cells use metabolic pathways to transfer energy; ATP is not simply another name for stored glucose.
