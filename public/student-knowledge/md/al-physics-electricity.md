## AL Physics: electricity, fields and modern physics: How do electric potential difference and current differ?

Potential difference is energy transferred per unit charge, measured in volts. Current is charge flow per unit time, measured in amperes. Confusing them can lead to incorrect explanations of series and parallel circuits.

## AL Physics: electricity, fields and modern physics: Two 6 Ω resistors are connected in parallel. What is their equivalent resistance?

1/R = 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/3, so R = 3 Ω. The equivalent resistance of positive resistors in parallel is smaller than the smallest branch resistance. Each branch has the same potential difference.

## AL Physics: electricity, fields and modern physics: What is the capacitance of a capacitor?

Capacitance C = Q/V, the charge magnitude on one plate divided by the potential difference. It is measured in farads. In an ideal capacitor, stored energy is ½CV². A capacitor is not a steady-current conducting path through its ideal dielectric.

## AL Physics: electricity, fields and modern physics: What does Faraday's law describe?

The induced electromotive force is related to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage. Lenz's law gives the opposing sense of the induced effect. State the chosen sign convention and distinguish flux from flux linkage when there are multiple turns.

## AL Physics: electricity, fields and modern physics: How is photon energy calculated?

E = hf = hc/λ for light in vacuum, using consistent units. A shorter wavelength corresponds to greater photon energy. Increasing the number of photons increases total delivered energy without necessarily changing the energy of each photon.

## AL Physics: electricity, fields and modern physics: What does half-life mean?

It is the time for the number of undecayed nuclei, or activity of a single isolated radioactive species, to fall to half its initial value. After two half-lives the expected remaining fraction is 1/4. Individual decays remain probabilistic.
