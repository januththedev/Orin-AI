## AL Chemistry: bonding, periodicity and qualitative analysis: How do ionic and covalent bonding differ in introductory models?

Ionic bonding involves electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. Covalent bonding involves shared electron density between atoms. Real bonding can have mixed character, so a simple classification is a model rather than an absolute boundary for every substance.

## AL Chemistry: bonding, periodicity and qualitative analysis: What does electronegativity describe?

It describes an atom's tendency to attract bonding electron density in a specified scale or model. It is not the same quantity as electron affinity or ionisation energy. Periodic trends should be explained with nuclear attraction, shielding and atomic structure.

## AL Chemistry: bonding, periodicity and qualitative analysis: What is oxidation number used for?

It is a formal electron-accounting tool for tracking oxidation and reduction. An increase indicates oxidation and a decrease reduction. Formal oxidation numbers are not necessarily actual charges on individual atoms in a covalent molecule.

## AL Chemistry: bonding, periodicity and qualitative analysis: How do I balance a redox process using half-equations?

Separate oxidation and reduction, balance relevant atoms and charge using the prescribed acidic or basic medium method, then equalise electron transfer and combine. Check both atom totals and overall charge. The medium matters for the final balanced equation.

## AL Chemistry: bonding, periodicity and qualitative analysis: Why must a qualitative-analysis observation be stated separately from an inference?

An observation records what was seen, such as a precipitate or colour change. An inference interprets it, such as suggesting an ion. A single test can be non-specific, so use the sequence of prescribed confirmatory evidence.

## AL Chemistry: bonding, periodicity and qualitative analysis: Can a flame colour alone conclusively identify an unknown sample?

Not always. Contamination, mixtures and observation conditions can obscure colours. Treat a flame test as evidence within an appropriate analytical scheme. Any practical chemical identification must follow teacher-supervised safety procedures.
