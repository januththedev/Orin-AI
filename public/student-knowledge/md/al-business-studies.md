## AL Business Studies: organisations and management: What is the difference between an objective and a strategy?

An objective states a desired result. A strategy outlines an approach to achieve it given resources and constraints. For example, increasing repeat customers is an objective; improving after-sales support is a possible strategy that needs evidence and evaluation.

## AL Business Studies: organisations and management: How do internal and external stakeholders differ?

Internal stakeholders participate within the organisation, while external stakeholders interact from outside it. Interests can overlap or conflict. Analyse a specific decision using the groups affected rather than assuming all stakeholders want the same outcome.

## AL Business Studies: organisations and management: What do the four Ps in a basic marketing mix represent?

Product, price, place and promotion. Apply each to a defined customer need and market. A list of advertising channels alone is not a complete marketing strategy, and the model is one framework rather than the only possible one.

## AL Business Studies: organisations and management: How is productivity calculated?

Productivity relates output to an input measure. If 200 units are produced in 20 labour-hours, labour productivity is 10 units per labour-hour. Comparisons require compatible definitions, quality and time periods.

## AL Business Studies: organisations and management: What distinguishes leadership from management in an introductory discussion?

Management commonly involves planning, organising and controlling resources. Leadership concerns influencing and guiding people toward shared purposes. Roles can overlap; a job title alone does not prove effective leadership.

## AL Business Studies: organisations and management: How do I evaluate a business decision in an essay?

State the decision and criteria, explain benefits and costs, consider stakeholders and constraints, and reach a supported judgement. Connect theory to the case evidence. Avoid an unconditional conclusion when success depends on assumptions.
