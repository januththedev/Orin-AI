## AL Physics: measurement, motion and mechanics: What is the difference between a scalar and a vector?

A scalar is specified by magnitude, while a vector also has direction and follows vector addition. Distance is scalar; displacement is vector. The numerical equality of their magnitudes in a particular motion does not make them the same quantity.

## AL Physics: measurement, motion and mechanics: What is the difference between precision and accuracy?

Precision concerns the closeness of repeated measurements or the measurement resolution, depending on context. Accuracy concerns closeness to a reference or true value. Repeating a measurement can reduce random uncertainty but does not automatically remove a systematic offset.

## AL Physics: measurement, motion and mechanics: A 0.2 kg ball moves at 10 m s⁻¹. What is its momentum?

Linear momentum p = mv = 0.2 × 10 = 2 kg m s⁻¹ in the direction of velocity. In a chosen one-dimensional axis, direction is represented by the sign.

## AL Physics: measurement, motion and mechanics: When is linear momentum conserved?

The total momentum of a system is conserved when the net external impulse is zero. Internal interaction forces cancel in the system balance. Kinetic energy need not be conserved in every collision even when total momentum is conserved.

## AL Physics: measurement, motion and mechanics: What is the work done by a constant force?

W = Fs cos θ, where θ is the angle between force and displacement. Work is negative when the force has a component opposing displacement. A force perpendicular to the displacement does no work in this model.

## AL Physics: measurement, motion and mechanics: How does centripetal acceleration differ from constant velocity?

Uniform circular motion has constant speed but changing velocity direction. Its acceleration has magnitude v²/r and points toward the centre. A resultant inward force is required; 'centripetal force' describes the required resultant, not an extra independent force.
