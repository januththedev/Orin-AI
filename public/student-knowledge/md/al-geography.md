## AL Geography: physical and human systems: How should a geographic explanation connect scale and process?

State whether the analysis is local, regional or global, identify relevant physical and human processes and explain their interaction. A local case study should not automatically be generalised to all of Sri Lanka or the world.

## AL Geography: physical and human systems: How do population growth and migration differ?

Population change can be analysed through births, deaths, immigration and emigration. Migration concerns movement with a change of usual residence under the definition used. Compare rates and totals only when their time periods and denominators match.

## AL Geography: physical and human systems: What is the difference between a hazard, exposure and vulnerability?

A hazard is a potentially damaging event or process. Exposure concerns people or assets in affected places; vulnerability concerns susceptibility to harm. Disaster risk depends on their interaction, not simply on whether a hazard exists.

## AL Geography: physical and human systems: How can a GIS help a geographic investigation?

It can combine spatially referenced layers, analyse relationships and present maps. Results depend on data quality, coordinate systems, scale and analytical choices. A visually detailed map is not automatically an accurate or unbiased one.

## AL Geography: physical and human systems: How should a climate graph be interpreted?

Read the temperature and rainfall axes and units, identify seasonal patterns, and compare the same type of statistic across locations. A monthly rainfall total and an average temperature represent different measures; do not interpret the curves as if they share one unit.

## AL Geography: physical and human systems: What makes a development indicator useful?

It should have a clear definition, relevant coverage, reliable measurement and an appropriate interpretation. Income, education, health and inequality describe different aspects. Use multiple indicators and a stated year rather than ranking places from one unexplained figure.
