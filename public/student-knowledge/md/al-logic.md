## AL Logic and Scientific Method: How do validity and truth differ in deductive logic?

Validity concerns whether the conclusion must follow if the premises are true. Truth concerns whether statements match the relevant facts or interpretation. A valid argument can contain false premises; soundness requires validity and true premises.

## AL Logic and Scientific Method: What is modus ponens?

From 'If P then Q' and P, infer Q. For example, if a number is divisible by 4 then it is even; a chosen number is divisible by 4; therefore it is even. The form differs from the invalid inference from Q back to P.

## AL Logic and Scientific Method: Why is affirming the consequent invalid?

From 'If P then Q' and Q, P does not necessarily follow because Q may have other sufficient causes. If rain makes a road wet and the road is wet, rain is not proved; a water spill is another possibility.

## AL Logic and Scientific Method: What makes a hypothesis testable?

It must imply observations that can in principle be checked under defined conditions. Specify variables, expected patterns and possible contrary evidence. A vague claim compatible with every possible result is difficult to evaluate scientifically.

## AL Logic and Scientific Method: How do deduction and induction differ?

Deduction aims at a conclusion guaranteed by the premises in a valid form. Induction reasons beyond the observed cases and is typically defeasible. Strong inductive evidence can support a conclusion without making it logically certain.

## AL Logic and Scientific Method: What is an operational definition?

It defines how a concept will be observed or measured in a particular investigation. For example, define 'study time' as logged focused minutes rather than an undefined impression. A measurement procedure should be relevant and reproducible.
