## AL Economics: markets, macroeconomics and policy: What is scarcity in economics?

Scarcity describes limited resources relative to competing wants, making choices necessary. Opportunity cost is the next-best alternative forgone. Scarcity is not identical to poverty, and even a wealthy society faces resource-allocation choices.

## AL Economics: markets, macroeconomics and policy: How do a movement along demand and a shift in demand differ?

A change in the good's own price produces a movement along a given demand curve, other things equal. A change in another demand determinant, such as income or preferences, can shift the curve. State the assumptions before predicting an outcome.

## AL Economics: markets, macroeconomics and policy: What is price elasticity of demand?

It measures responsiveness of quantity demanded to a price change, commonly expressed as percentage change in quantity divided by percentage change in price. Explain whether the signed value or absolute magnitude is being used and which calculation method the task requires.

## AL Economics: markets, macroeconomics and policy: How do nominal and real GDP differ?

Nominal GDP values output using prices for the relevant current period. Real GDP adjusts for price changes using an appropriate price basis or volume measure. Growth in nominal GDP alone does not establish the same growth in output volume.

## AL Economics: markets, macroeconomics and policy: What is inflation?

Inflation is an increase in the general price level over a period, measured using a specified index. One product becoming more expensive is not enough to establish general inflation. A lower positive inflation rate means prices are rising more slowly, not necessarily falling.

## AL Economics: markets, macroeconomics and policy: How do fiscal and monetary policy differ?

Fiscal policy concerns government taxation, spending and related financing. Monetary policy concerns central-bank actions affecting monetary and financial conditions. Effects depend on the economy and policy design; this foundation pack does not state current Sri Lankan rates or predict investment returns.
