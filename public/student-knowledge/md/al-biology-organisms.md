## AL Biology: plant and animal physiology: How do xylem and phloem differ?

Xylem conducts water and dissolved mineral ions and also provides support. Phloem transports organic solutes between sources and sinks. Source and sink relationships can change, so do not define phloem transport as always downward.

## AL Biology: plant and animal physiology: How do transpiration and translocation differ?

Transpiration is water-vapour loss, largely through stomata in leaves. Translocation is movement of organic solutes through phloem. Their mechanisms and substances differ, although both are part of whole-plant transport.

## AL Biology: plant and animal physiology: What is homeostasis?

It is regulation of aspects of the internal environment within functional ranges despite disturbances. Negative feedback is a common mechanism. A regulated variable can fluctuate around a reference range; homeostasis does not mean every body condition is perfectly constant.

## AL Biology: plant and animal physiology: How do an artery and a vein differ?

Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it toward the heart. Oxygenation is not their defining distinction: pulmonary arteries and veins are important counterexamples to the common shortcut.

## AL Biology: plant and animal physiology: What is the role of a nephron in school-level kidney physiology?

A nephron contributes to filtration, selective reabsorption and secretion in forming urine and regulating internal conditions. Separate the movement of water and solutes through different processes. This physiological description is not a medical diagnosis.

## AL Biology: plant and animal physiology: How do nervous and endocrine coordination differ?

Nervous coordination uses electrical and chemical signalling in neural pathways; endocrine coordination uses hormones carried to target cells, often through blood. Speed and duration vary, so avoid treating every nervous effect as instant and every hormonal effect as slow.
