## AL Physics: waves, optics and thermal physics: How are wave speed, frequency and wavelength related?

For a periodic wave, v = fλ. If f = 50 Hz and λ = 0.4 m, v = 20 m s⁻¹. When a wave crosses into another medium, the source frequency is generally unchanged while speed and wavelength can change.

## AL Physics: waves, optics and thermal physics: What distinguishes transverse and longitudinal waves?

In a transverse wave, oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of propagation. In a longitudinal wave, oscillations are parallel. A wave transports energy; the medium's particles need not travel with the disturbance over its full distance.

## AL Physics: waves, optics and thermal physics: What is required for a stable interference pattern?

The overlapping waves need a consistent phase relationship and compatible frequency. Path difference determines whether contributions reinforce or oppose. The resulting intensity depends on amplitudes as well as phase, so complete cancellation requires equal opposing amplitudes.

## AL Physics: waves, optics and thermal physics: State Snell's law and its angle convention.

n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂, where angles are measured from the normal to the interface. Total internal reflection requires travel from higher to lower refractive index and an incidence angle above the critical angle.

## AL Physics: waves, optics and thermal physics: How much heat raises 0.5 kg of water by 10 K if c = 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹?

Using Q = mcΔT gives Q = 0.5 × 4200 × 10 = 21,000 J. This assumes no phase change and neglects heat loss and the container's heat capacity. The supplied value of c is part of the example.

## AL Physics: waves, optics and thermal physics: How are temperature and internal energy different?

Temperature characterises thermal state and determines the direction of spontaneous heat transfer. Internal energy is the microscopic energy stored in a system. Objects at the same temperature can have different internal energies because their amounts and materials differ.
